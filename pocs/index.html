BoardHub design explorations

Three directions (A to C), then three variations on Parlour (B1 to B3). Each shows the front page and a live chess match with the resign question. On every match page, the State buttons at the bottom switch between your turn, waiting, the resign prompt and both endings.

A. Ebony

Dark luxury. Warm ebony, cream text, one brass accent. The status becomes a large serif headline instead of a pill, and the front page opens on a board lit by a single piece.

Front pageMatch

B. Parlour

Cozy. Peach ground, thick brown outlines on everything, sticker shadows, rounded type. The board sits in a window and resign is a dialog. Icons are drawn as SVG, no icon set.

Front pageMatch

C. Drafting table

Illustrated. Cool paper, navy ink, cobalt and marigold. The front page draws the idea (one engine, many boards) as a moving isometric picture, and a resigned king tips over.

Front pageMatch

Around B

Three ways of pushing Parlour further. Each one links B's own stylesheet and builds on it, so they stay recognisably the same family.

B1. Desktop

The whole app as a cozy desktop. Game folders are icons, lobbies and the match are windows you can drag (by mouse or arrow keys), and the resign question is its own little window.

Open the desktop

B2. Friends

Every piece is a hand-drawn character with a face. They blink, hop when picked, look dizzy when captured, and your king waves a white flag. Adds the waiting room, animal avatars and four colourways.

Front pageMatch

B3. Scrapbook

A handmade page: washi tape, sticky notes, polaroids, name tags, ticket stubs and handwriting. Resigning slams a stamp onto the board. Now with the real cover images (mounted prints, polaroids, a missing-photo fallback).

Front pageMatch

Round 3

B4. Study

Parlour's shapes in chess-set woods: walnut, chocolate or boxwood (switch at the bottom left). Leads with the game builder, a working board-shape tool with the unbuilt parts taped off. Covers are small toned prints on each game folder.

Front pageMatch

Round 4: the real app's look, rethought

Unlike the rounds above, these run the real engine and the app's own Walnut stylesheet. Start them with pocs/d-play-first/serve.sh (they need HTTP and make wasm).

D. Play-first table

The front page opens on a live, playable board with a strip to swap games, then the catalogue as an organised list instead of folder cards. A game page with its board beside the title, a dedicated rules page, and a dice tray that shows what was rolled.
